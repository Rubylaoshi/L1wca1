Part A · Sentence Structure Progression

Chinese Grammar: From Basic (Level 1) to Complex (Level 5)

Every rule and example below uses strictly your compulsory syllabus vocabulary in Pinyin & British English.

LEVEL 1 · BASIC

Adjectival Predicates (No “shì”!)

In Chinese, adjectives (hǎo, máng) act directly as the verb/predicate. Never put shì (to be) before an adjective! Use hěn (very) for positive statements and bù (not) for negatives.

Pattern: Subject + (hěn / bù) + Adjective

• Wǒ hěn hǎo. — I am very well. (NOT: Wǒ shì hěn hǎo)

• Tā hěn máng. — He is very busy.

• Wǒ bù máng. — I am not busy.

LEVEL 2 · ELEMENTARY

Core S + V + O & “huí jiā” vs “qù”

Basic action sentences follow Subject + Verb + Object. For places, use zài (to be at/in) and qù (to go to), except for home (jiā) which must pair with huí (to return).

Pattern: Subject + Verb + Object

• Wǒ zài sùshè. — I am in the dormitory. / Wǒ bú zài jiā. — I am not at home.

• Tā qù túshūguǎn. — He is going to the library.

• Wǒ huí jiā. — I return home. (NEVER: Wǒ qù jiā)

• Wǒ hē kāfēi. / Tā chī hànbāo. — I drink coffee. / He eats a hamburger.

LEVEL 3 · INTERMEDIATE

Adverb Placement & Stacking (yě, dōu, hěn, bù)

Adverbs always stand after the Subject and before the Verb or Adjective. When stacking adverbs, follow: yě (also) → dōu (both/all) → hěn / bù (very / not).

Pattern: Subject + [yě] + [dōu] + [hěn / bù] + Verb / Adj.

• Wǒ yě hěn hǎo. — I am also very well.

• Tāmen dōu hěn hǎo. — They are all very well.

• Wǒmen yě dōu hē kāfēi. — We also all drink coffee.

• Tāmen dōu bú qù shāngchǎng. — None of them are going to the shopping mall.

LEVEL 4 · UPPER-INTERMEDIATE

Three Question Types (ma, ne, nǎr/shénme)

Never mix question markers! Use ma for yes-no questions, ne for bounce-back questions, and keep nǎr / nǎlǐ / shénme in the Object slot with no “ma”.

• 1. Yes-No (Statement + ma): Nǐ jiějie qù yīyuàn ma? — Is your elder sister going to the hospital?

• 2. Bounce-Back (Noun/Pronoun + ne): Wǒ bù máng, nǐ nán péngyou ne? — I'm not busy, and your boyfriend?

• 3. Wh-Questions (nǎr / nǎlǐ / shénme): Nǐ dìdi zài nǎr? / Nǐ yào chī shénme? (NEVER add “ma”)

LEVEL 5 · COMPLEX

Full 6-Slot Multi-Verb & Time Sentences

In complex sentences, Time (jīntiān, míngtiān, zuótiān, zǎoshang, xiàwǔ, wǎnshang, xīngqī...) comes before (or right after) the Subject, and auxiliary/mental verbs (yào to want, xǐhuan to like) precede the main action verb (chī, hē, kàn, qù) + Object.

Time + Subject + Adverb + Verb 1 + Verb 2 + Object

• Jīntiān wǎnshang tā mèimei xǐhuan kàn diànyǐng.
Tonight her younger sister likes watching films.

• Míngtiān zǎoshang wǒmen dōu yào qù dàxué.
Tomorrow morning we all want to go to the university.

Negation & Questions in Complex Multi-Slot Sentences

• Tā yě bú yào chī hànbāo.
He also doesn't want to eat hamburgers.

• Xīngqī liù nǐmen dōu qù diànyǐngyuàn ma?
On Saturday are you all going to the cinema?

Part B · Exam Pinyin & Tone-Marking Rules

Vowel Priority, Neutral Tones, Sandhi & Numbers (0–10,000)

1

Vowel Tone-Mark Priority

When a syllable has two or more vowels, place the tone mark on the vowel with the widest mouth opening:

a > o > e > i > u > ü
  • • kāfēi: a wins in kā; e beats i in fēi (never kāfeī).
  • • túshūguǎn: a beats u in guǎn (never gǔan).
  • • jiàoshì / diànyǐngyuàn: a always wins in jiào, diàn, and yuàn.
  • • Special Rule (-iu & -ui): When i and u appear together, mark the second vowel: liù (6), jiǔ (9), duìbuqi (duì marks i), huí (huí marks i).
  • • Dot Rule on “i” & “ü”: Drop the dot above i when adding a tone mark (nǐ, lì, yī). Drop the two dots on ü after j, q, x, y (qù, dàxué, yuàn, Yǔ).
2

Neutral Tone (0) Exam Words

Neutral-tone syllables are unstressed and must be written with no tone mark in written exams:

Family & Pronouns

bàba (dad), māma (mom)

gēge (elder brother), jiějie (elder sister)

dìdi (younger brother), mèimei (younger sister)

wǒmen, nǐmen, tāmen, men

Particles & Daily Words

ma (yes-no question), ne (bounce-back question)

xièxie (thank you), bú kèqi (you're welcome)

duìbuqi (sorry — bu & qi neutral), méi guānxi (it's alright)

péngyou (friend), xǐhuan (like), shénme (what)

zǎoshang (morning), wǎnshang (evening)

3

Spoken vs Written Tone Sandhi

A. Two 3rd Tones (3rd + 3rd → spoken 2nd + 3rd)

In written exams, keep both 3rd tone marks even though you speak the first one as 2nd tone:

Write: nǐ hǎo (spoken ní hǎo) · Write: wǒ hěn hǎo (spoken wó hén hǎo)

B. Tone Change of “bù” & “yī”

Before a 4th-tone syllable, bù changes to 2nd tone bú:

• bú qù (not going) · bú yào (don't want) · bú shì (is not) · bú kèqi (you're welcome)

• Before 1st/2nd/3rd tone, keep 4th tone: bù máng, bù xǐhuan, bù hē

• yī changes to 4th tone yì before 1st/2nd/3rd tone (yìbǎi 100, yìqiān 1,000), and changes to 2nd tone yí before 4th tone (yíwàn 10,000 — 2nd tone yí before 4th-tone wàn).

4

Number Place-Value Units & 0–10,000 Rules

  • • 0–9: 0 líng, 1 yī, 2 èr (or liǎng before measure words / hundreds), 3 sān, 4 sì, 5 wǔ, 6 liù, 7 qī, 8 bā, 9 jiǔ.
  • • Underlined Place-Value Units (1 Syllable Each): 10 (ten) = shí, 100 (hundred) = bǎi (NOT yìbǎi), 1000 (thousand) = qiān (NOT yìqiān), 10000 (ten thousand) = wàn (NOT yíwàn).
  • • 11–19 (Joined Compound Words): Start directly with shí: 11 shíyī, 12 shí'èr (apostrophe before èr), 16 shíliù, 19 shíjiǔ.
  • • 20–99 (Joined Compound Words): 20 èrshí, 21 èrshíyī, 32 sānshí'èr, 98 jiǔshíbā.
  • • 100–120: 100 (one hundred) = yìbǎi; 101–109 insert líng (103 yìbǎi líng sān); 110–119 keep yī before shí (110 yìbǎi yīshí, 112 yìbǎi yīshí'èr); 120 yìbǎi èrshí.
  • • 200, 1,000, 2,000 & 10,000: 200 = èrbǎi / liǎngbǎi; 1,000 (one thousand) = yìqiān; 2,000 = èrqiān / liǎngqiān; 10,000 (ten thousand) = yíwàn (2nd tone yí before 4th-tone wàn).
